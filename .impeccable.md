# Impeccable Design Guidelines

## Design Context

### Users
VoIP Accelerator serves **telecom carriers, wholesalers, and rate-deck analysts** — the people who buy and sell minutes in the VoIP market. Their job-to-be-done is comparing rate decks (A-Z international and US NPANXX), running LERG lookups, spotting pricing/coverage gaps, and exporting corrected rate sheets. Today they often do this in Excel; the product replaces that with fast, side-by-side analysis that handles 250K+ lines.

Context of use: focused, analytical work — often under time pressure, evaluating margin opportunities. They care about **accuracy, speed, and data privacy** (data stays local in the browser). The product is **free forever**, so the landing page's job is to build credibility and drive sign-up, not to justify a price.

### Brand Personality
- **Three words:** Refined, trustworthy, precise.
- **Voice/tone:** Confident and clear, not loud. Speak to professionals as peers. Let the product's capability and the privacy story carry the message rather than hype or aggression.
- **Emotional goal:** The interface should evoke **confidence and calm competence** — "this is a serious, well-built tool I can trust with my data and my margins."
- **Shift from today:** Moving away from the current edgy/aggressive treatment ("CRUSH YOUR BUSINESS CASES", glitch logo, scramble text) toward enterprise-SaaS poise.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** Dark mode (retain). The product UI is dark; the marketing page should feel continuous with it.
- **Palette:** Open for reinvention. The legacy accent is emerald/teal green `hsl(160 100% 40%)`; the facelift may keep, refine, or replace it. Explore palettes that read as premium and trustworthy (deep neutrals + a confident accent; consider richer/cooler accents alongside the green).
- **Typography:** Inter for UI/sans; a monospace (Roboto Mono / PT Mono) is on-brand for telecom/data moments — use it intentionally for numbers, codes, and labels rather than everywhere.
- **References to explore (no single winner chosen — span the range):**
  - *Linear / Vercel* — sleek dev-tool: tight type, generous negative space, subtle gradients, restrained motion.
  - *Stripe / Plaid* — polished fintech-enterprise: structured, diagrammatic, trustworthy, product visuals.
  - *Terminal / data-native* — monospace, grid lines, dashboard/ASCII motifs that speak to the engineer audience.
- **Anti-references / explicitly NOT:** Chaotic glitch effects, scramble/jitter text, shouty all-caps slogans, gimmicky motion. Nothing that undermines a sense of reliability.
- **Motion:** Subtle and purposeful only — tasteful entrance fades and hover states. Must respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. No flashy or distracting animation.

### Design Principles
1. **Credibility over hype.** Every element should reinforce "trustworthy, well-engineered tool." Show the product and the proof, don't shout claims.
2. **Clarity through hierarchy and space.** Use whitespace, restrained type scale, and clear visual order. Let the page breathe.
3. **Data-native, tastefully.** Lean into the telecom/analyst identity with monospace numerals, real comparison/coverage visuals, and precise labeling — without turning the whole page into a terminal.
4. **Privacy & "free forever" are headline assets.** Local-only data and zero cost are differentiators — surface them as trust signals, not footnotes.
5. **Motion serves meaning.** Animate to guide attention or reveal structure, subtly, and always degrade gracefully for reduced-motion users.

## Messaging

Locked positioning for the landing page (synthesized from the messaging brain-dump + the US-NPANXX-focus product decision). Use this as the source of truth for HomeView copy.

### Scope — US NPANXX only
- The product is positioned as **US NPANXX rate intelligence only.** A-Z (international) is intentionally dropped from all marketing copy — it can't currently be validated, so we don't market it. Don't reintroduce A-Z, "breakouts", "dial codes", or international destinations into copy.
- Vocabulary to USE: NPANXX, NPA, NXX, LERG, rate deck, jurisdiction (interstate / intrastate / indeterminate), margin, buying/selling, switch-ready. Keep telecom terms precise — the audience are peers.

### One-liner
There is hidden knowledge in your rate decks; this exposes it. "See the margin hiding in your rate decks."

### The three jobs (the spine of the page)
1. **Compare** — two NPANXX decks side-by-side: coverage/code gaps and rate deltas by state, NPA, or individual NPANXX, split by jurisdiction.
2. **Adjust** — reprice or build a deck down to the code: by %, fixed amount, a single NPANXX, every NPA in a state, or a metro by population; set effective dates.
3. **Export** — switch-ready sheets plus downloadable comparison reports.

### Differentiators (lead with these, as trust signals not footnotes)
- **Local-first & ephemeral** — parsing runs in the browser; nothing is stored; close the tab and the data is gone. This is also a trust promise from an operator in the same business ("not a backdoor to see your rates").
- **LERG-enriched & jurisdiction-aware** — every NPANXX carries geography + interstate/intrastate/indeterminate context that's painful to track in Excel.
- **Free forever** — a wedge and a trust signal, not a price to justify.
- **Built for full-size decks** — handles 200K+ rows where spreadsheets/pivot tables fall down.

### Voice
- **Refined, trustworthy, precise** — confident peer-to-peer, not loud. Credibility over hype.
- One deliberate, light **"cheat code"** accent at the close ("the cheat code for buying and selling smarter"). Don't overuse it; it's a wink, not the whole voice.
- **Kill:** "CRUSH YOUR BUSINESS CASES", glitch/scramble effects, shouty all-caps slogans, real-time/network-monitoring framing (it is NOT a switch/monitoring tool).

### Proof — qualitative only
- No fabricated customer logos and no hard performance numbers. Speak to speed qualitatively ("load a deck in seconds; a full two-deck analysis in minutes") and use an honest **"built by an operator"** band instead of fake social proof.
